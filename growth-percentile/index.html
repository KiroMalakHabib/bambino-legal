Baby growth percentile calculator

Enter your baby's weight, length and head size to see their percentile on the WHO growth charts, the standard used by doctors in most countries for children from birth to 5 years.

What does a percentile mean?

A percentile compares your baby with healthy babies of the same age and sex. If your baby's weight is on the 40th percentile, about 40 out of 100 babies weigh less and about 60 weigh more.

A higher percentile is not "better". A baby on the 15th percentile and a baby on the 85th percentile can both be growing perfectly well. Small parents often have small babies, and big parents often have big babies.

What is a normal percentile?

Most growth charts mark the 3rd and 97th percentiles as the edges of the usual range (some countries use the 2nd and 98th). Most healthy babies sit somewhere between them.

PercentileWhat it usually means
3rd to 97thWithin the usual range
Below the 3rdSmaller than most babies the same age. Your doctor may want to check it.
Above the 97thBigger than most babies the same age. Your doctor may want to check it.

What matters most is the trend. Babies usually follow roughly the same curve over time. A steady 10th percentile is often less worrying than a baby who drops from the 75th to the 25th.

When to talk to your doctor

  • Weight, length or head size is below the 3rd or above the 97th percentile.
  • The percentile changes a lot between checks, for example crossing two of the main lines on the chart (such as from the 50th to below the 15th).
  • Your newborn has not regained their birth weight by about 2 weeks.
  • You are worried about feeding, wet diapers, energy or development, whatever the chart says.

Why WHO growth charts?

The WHO Child Growth Standards come from the WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study, which followed healthy, mostly breastfed children in Brazil, Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and the United States. They show how children grow when they get a healthy start, which is why most countries use them from birth to 5 years. In the United States, the CDC recommends the WHO charts for children under 2.

This calculator uses WHO's official daily tables, so it gives a precise result for your baby's exact age in days, not just their age in months.

How to measure at home

  • Weight: weigh without clothes or a diaper, on the same scale each time. With a bathroom scale, weigh yourself holding the baby, then subtract your own weight.
  • Length: before age 2, measure lying down, from the top of the head to the heel, with the leg gently straightened. After 2, measure standing.
  • Head circumference: wrap a soft tape around the widest part of the head, just above the eyebrows and ears.

Home measurements are less exact than at the clinic, so small differences are normal. Use them to follow the trend.

Premature babies

If your baby was born early, doctors usually use the corrected age (age from the due date, not the birth date) until about 2 years. To do the same here, enter the due date as the date of birth.
