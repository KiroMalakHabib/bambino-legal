All of these are free at government hospitals, health centres and Anganwadi immunisation sessions. Keep your baby's Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card and bring it to every visit.

Polio injections: since January 2023 the government gives three fractional polio injections (fIPV): at 6 weeks, 14 weeks and 9 months, with the MR vaccine. Some older printed charts still show only two.

What each vaccine protects against

  • BCG: protects against severe forms of tuberculosis in young children. It leaves a small scar on the arm, which is normal.
  • Hepatitis B: protects the liver. The first dose is given within 24 hours of birth; later doses are part of the pentavalent vaccine.
  • Pentavalent: one injection that protects against 5 diseases: diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough), tetanus, hepatitis B and Hib.
  • Polio (OPV drops and fIPV injections): the drops and the small injections work together to protect against polio.
  • Rotavirus: drops by mouth that protect against severe diarrhoea.
  • Pneumococcal (PCV): protects against pneumonia, meningitis and ear infections.
  • Measles-Rubella (MR): protects against measles and rubella.
  • Japanese Encephalitis (JE): given only in districts where JE is common.
  • Vitamin A: not a vaccine, but given with the 9-month vaccines and then every 6 months until age 5 to support immunity and eyesight.

Extra vaccines at private clinics (IAP)

Many paediatricians follow the Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) schedule, which adds vaccines that are not part of the free government programme, such as:

  • MMR (adds mumps protection) instead of MR
  • Typhoid conjugate vaccine at 6–9 months
  • Flu vaccine from 6 months, every year until 5 years
  • Hepatitis A from 12 months
  • Chickenpox (varicella) from 15 months

These are paid. Ask your paediatrician which ones make sense for your child.

After the vaccine: what's normal?

Some redness or swelling where the injection was given, a mild fever, or being fussy or sleepy for a day or two is normal. Keep feeding as usual and give lots of cuddles.

Call the doctor if the fever is very high, your baby cries unusually for hours, has trouble breathing or a swollen face, or seems unwell in a way that worries you.

Missed a vaccine?

You don't need to start again. Visit your health centre as soon as you can to catch up on the missed doses.
